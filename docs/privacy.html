PrepareMe Privacy

Last changed: November 23, 2026

PrepareMe is an offline preparedness reference for iPhone and iPad. The developer collects nothing from it: there is no account or sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no server of the developer's that the app sends anything to.

What stays on your device

Your library of guides and maps, your household inventory, the pins you save on the map, your searches, your game progress and your settings stay on the device. Essential Documents are kept encrypted on the device and open with Face ID or your passcode.

What goes to your own Mac

If you pair the app with the PrepareMe companion on a Mac you run, it talks to that Mac over your home network only. It sends the Mac your inventory changes and the pins you add, with the name you gave this device; counts of how well the app opened, and of any crashes or freezes; and this device's free space, only if you say yes when asked (you can change that in Settings). That Mac is yours. None of it reaches the developer.

What the app asks public services

Only when you use the feature, the app asks a public service a question and shows the answer:

  • Barcode lookups. When you scan a product's barcode, its number goes to Open Food Facts, Open Beauty Facts, Open Products Facts, UPCitemdb, the U.S. Department of Agriculture's FoodData Central, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration's drug list or UPC Database, to find the product's name.
  • Weather and conditions. On a paired device, the app reads weather alerts and forecasts from the U.S. National Weather Service, river levels from NOAA and the storm outlook from the National Hurricane Center, for the area your own library names, and the power-outage and evacuation pages your library names.
  • Your location. If you allow location and are away from home, your location, rounded to about a kilometer, goes to the National Weather Service to get the forecast and alerts for where you are. It is never kept by the app or sent to your Mac. The map shows where you are on the device itself.

Nothing that identifies you goes with these questions. What reaches each service is covered by that service's own privacy terms; the developer has no arrangement with any of them and receives nothing from them.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Camera: to read a pairing code, read barcodes and scan papers.
  • Location while using the app: to show where you are on the map and the weather where you are.
  • Local network: to reach your Mac at home.
  • Face ID: to open Essential Documents.
  • Notifications: reminders when stored food is near its date, and the app's own notices.

Children

The app collects nothing from anyone, children included.
